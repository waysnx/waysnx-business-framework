# WBF-DOC-0075 – Knowledge Management Architecture

## Purpose

This specification defines the enterprise Knowledge Management Architecture within the WaysNX Business Framework (WBF). It establishes the architectural principles, logical components, governance model, lifecycle, and operational practices required to manage enterprise knowledge as a strategic organizational asset for both human users and Artificial Intelligence systems.

Knowledge Management Architecture enables organizations to capture, organize, govern, enrich, discover, and continuously improve enterprise knowledge while supporting AI Agents, Generative AI, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), intelligent automation, and business decision-making.

This specification is technology independent and applies to enterprise documentation, business knowledge, technical knowledge, policies, procedures, product documentation, operational knowledge, and AI-enabled knowledge systems.

# 2. Definitions

### Knowledge

Validated information that provides business value through experience, documentation, processes, policies, or expertise.

### Knowledge Base

A structured repository containing enterprise knowledge accessible by people and AI systems.

### Knowledge Asset

Any reusable organizational information including documents, procedures, specifications, manuals, templates, code samples, FAQs, or business rules.

### Metadata

Descriptive information used to classify, organize, search, secure, and govern knowledge assets.

### Knowledge Graph

A structured representation of relationships between enterprise concepts, entities, documents, and business capabilities.

# 11. Best Practices

- Maintain a single source of truth.
- Classify knowledge consistently.
- Capture metadata automatically where possible.
- Link related knowledge assets.
- Keep documentation modular and reusable.
- Version every significant knowledge asset.
- Validate knowledge before publication.
- Continuously review outdated information.
- Design knowledge for both humans and AI.

---

# 12. Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- Duplicate documentation
- Isolated knowledge silos
- Missing metadata
- Outdated documentation
- Hardcoded business knowledge
- Uncontrolled knowledge repositories
- Poor search capabilities
- Missing ownership
- AI consuming ungoverned knowledge
