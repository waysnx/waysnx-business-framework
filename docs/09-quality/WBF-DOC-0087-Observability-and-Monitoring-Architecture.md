# WBF-DOC-0087 – Observability & Monitoring Architecture

## Purpose

This specification defines the enterprise Observability & Monitoring Architecture within the WaysNX Business Framework (WBF). It establishes the architectural principles, operational intelligence capabilities, telemetry standards, monitoring models, alerting strategies, and continuous feedback mechanisms required to ensure enterprise systems remain measurable, diagnosable, reliable, and continuously improvable throughout their operational lifecycle.

Observability extends traditional monitoring by enabling engineering teams to understand the internal behavior of complex systems through telemetry, enabling rapid diagnosis, proactive optimization, and data-driven operational decision making.

This specification is technology independent and applies to enterprise applications, APIs, cloud platforms, AI-enabled systems, infrastructure, integrations, and digital services.

# 2. Definitions

### Observability

The capability to understand the internal state and behavior of enterprise systems through telemetry generated by those systems.

### Monitoring

The continuous measurement of predefined indicators to detect expected operational conditions.

### Telemetry

Operational data generated by systems including metrics, logs, traces, events, and health information.

### Distributed Tracing

Tracking requests as they traverse multiple services to understand execution paths, latency, and failures.

### Operational Intelligence

The analysis of operational data to support engineering decisions, service optimization, and business reliability.

# 14. Best Practices

- Instrument systems from the beginning.
- Standardize telemetry across platforms.
- Correlate logs, metrics, and traces.
- Design meaningful dashboards.
- Minimize alert fatigue.
- Continuously review operational data.
- Measure customer experience.
- Automate operational reporting.
- Continuously improve observability maturity.

---

# 15. Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- Monitoring without observability
- Excessive alert noise
- Missing telemetry standards
- Siloed dashboards
- Uncorrelated operational data
- Manual incident diagnosis
- Ignoring telemetry quality
- Short-term operational visibility only
- No operational analytics
