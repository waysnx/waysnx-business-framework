# WBF-DOC-0111 – Business Capability Reference Model

## Purpose

This specification defines the Business Capability Reference Model (BCRM) within the WaysNX Business Framework (WBF). It provides a standardized, technology-independent representation of the core business capabilities required to operate and grow an enterprise successfully.

The Business Capability Reference Model serves as the enterprise blueprint for understanding **what** an organization does, independent of **how** it performs those activities, **who** performs them, or **which technology** supports them.

The model establishes a common vocabulary for business leaders, enterprise architects, solution architects, product managers, delivery teams, and governance bodies, enabling strategic planning, digital transformation, capability assessment, investment prioritization, and organizational alignment.

This specification applies to organizations of all sizes and industries and serves as the foundation for business architecture throughout the WaysNX Business Framework.

# 2. Definitions

### Business Capability

A business capability is the ability of an organization to achieve a business outcome through people, processes, information, technology, and governance.

Business capabilities describe **what** an enterprise does, not **how** it performs the work.

---

### Capability Map

A hierarchical representation of enterprise business capabilities.

---

### Capability Domain

A logical grouping of related business capabilities serving a common business function.

---

### Capability Owner

The individual or organizational unit responsible for the effectiveness and maturity of a capability.

---

### Capability Maturity

The measurable effectiveness of a business capability in consistently delivering business value.

# 4. Business Capability Principles

Business capabilities should be:

- Business Driven
- Technology Independent
- Stable
- Reusable
- Measurable
- Value Focused
- Modular
- Scalable
- Governed
- Continuously Improved

Capabilities represent enduring business abilities rather than organizational structures, applications, or business processes.

# 11. Best Practices

- Define capabilities independently of organizational structure.
- Avoid technology-specific capability definitions.
- Maintain consistent capability hierarchies.
- Assign clear ownership.
- Measure capability maturity.
- Align capabilities with strategy.
- Review capabilities periodically.
- Reuse capability definitions across projects.
- Integrate capability maps into enterprise architecture.

---

# 12. Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- Confusing capabilities with business processes
- Defining capabilities around applications
- Organizationally dependent capability maps
- Excessively detailed capability decomposition
- Duplicate capabilities
- Missing capability ownership
- Technology-driven capability definitions
- Ignoring maturity assessments
- Static capability models
