# WBF-DOC-0104 – Policy Management

## Purpose

This specification defines the enterprise Policy Management Architecture within the WaysNX Business Framework (WBF). It establishes the principles, governance model, enterprise capabilities, lifecycle, and architectural framework required to create, approve, publish, maintain, enforce, review, and retire enterprise policies, standards, procedures, and governance documentation.

Policy Management ensures that enterprise governance artifacts remain consistent, current, traceable, enforceable, and aligned with organizational objectives, business strategy, regulatory obligations, enterprise architecture, and operational practices.

This specification is technology independent and applies across all business units, enterprise architecture domains, operational functions, digital products, technology platforms, governance bodies, and supporting enterprise services.

# 2. Definitions

### Policy

A formally approved statement defining mandatory enterprise expectations, governance requirements, or organizational rules.

### Standard

A mandatory specification establishing consistent enterprise practices supporting one or more policies.

### Procedure

A documented sequence of activities describing how approved policies and standards are implemented.

### Guideline

A recommended practice that assists implementation while allowing flexibility where appropriate.

### Policy Owner

The individual or governance body accountable for maintaining, reviewing, approving, and improving a policy.

# 11. Best Practices

- Maintain a centralized policy repository.
- Assign clear ownership for every policy.
- Define mandatory review cycles.
- Standardize policy templates.
- Use version control for governance artifacts.
- Communicate policy changes effectively.
- Measure policy adoption.
- Track policy exceptions.
- Continuously improve governance documentation.

---

# 12. Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- Policies without owners
- Outdated documentation
- Duplicate policies
- Inconsistent standards
- Uncontrolled policy changes
- Policies that are never reviewed
- Poor communication
- Missing version history
- Policies disconnected from business objectives
