# WBF-DOC-0103 – Compliance Architecture

## Purpose

This specification defines the enterprise Compliance Architecture within the WaysNX Business Framework (WBF). It establishes the principles, governance model, enterprise capabilities, architectural framework, and lifecycle required to ensure organizational compliance with internal policies, contractual commitments, regulatory obligations, industry standards, and enterprise governance requirements.

Compliance Architecture provides a structured approach for integrating compliance into enterprise strategy, business operations, technology platforms, digital products, information management, and operational processes while enabling transparency, accountability, and continuous organizational improvement.

This specification is technology independent and applies across all business units, enterprise architecture domains, operational functions, digital products, technology platforms, cloud services, AI-enabled systems, and supporting enterprise services.

# 2. Definitions

### Compliance

The state of adhering to applicable laws, regulations, standards, contractual obligations, organizational policies, and governance requirements.

### Compliance Architecture

The enterprise framework defining how compliance capabilities, governance structures, processes, controls, and monitoring activities are integrated across the organization.

### Compliance Control

A policy, process, procedure, technical safeguard, or operational activity implemented to satisfy a compliance requirement.

### Compliance Assessment

A structured evaluation used to determine whether enterprise activities comply with defined requirements.

### Compliance Evidence

Documentation, records, logs, reports, or artifacts demonstrating adherence to compliance obligations.

# 11. Best Practices

- Define enterprise-wide compliance ownership.
- Standardize compliance controls.
- Automate evidence collection where possible.
- Continuously monitor compliance posture.
- Integrate compliance into engineering processes.
- Maintain centralized compliance documentation.
- Review compliance regularly.
- Measure control effectiveness.
- Continuously improve compliance maturity.

---

# 12. Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- Compliance only during audits
- Manual evidence collection
- Undefined control ownership
- Siloed compliance activities
- Ignoring policy violations
- Poor documentation
- Reactive compliance management
- Inconsistent controls
- Compliance disconnected from business operations
