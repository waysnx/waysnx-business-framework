# WBF-DOC-0108 – Decision Governance

## Purpose

This specification defines the Decision Governance Architecture within the WaysNX Business Framework (WBF). It establishes the governance model, principles, decision authorities, enterprise capabilities, architectural framework, and lifecycle required to ensure that organizational decisions are consistent, transparent, accountable, evidence-based, and aligned with enterprise strategy.

Decision Governance provides a structured framework for defining decision rights, governance workflows, approval mechanisms, escalation models, and accountability across business operations, enterprise architecture, technology platforms, digital products, operational services, programs, projects, and governance bodies.

This specification is technology independent and applies across all business units, enterprise architecture domains, digital products, operational functions, technology initiatives, governance committees, and executive leadership.

# 2. Definitions

### Decision Governance

The enterprise framework defining how organizational decisions are made, approved, communicated, implemented, reviewed, and continuously improved.

### Decision Authority

The individual, committee, or governance body authorized to approve specific categories of decisions.

### Decision Rights

Clearly defined responsibilities identifying who is responsible, accountable, consulted, and informed for organizational decisions.

### Governance Board

A formal decision-making body responsible for strategic, architectural, operational, financial, or technology oversight.

### Escalation

The structured process of transferring decision authority when predefined thresholds or conditions are met.

# 11. Best Practices

- Clearly define decision ownership.
- Standardize approval workflows.
- Maintain documented decision records.
- Base decisions on measurable evidence.
- Review strategic decisions regularly.
- Define escalation thresholds.
- Empower governance boards.
- Measure decision effectiveness.
- Continuously improve governance processes.

---

# 12. Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- Undefined decision authority
- Decisions without accountability
- Excessive approval layers
- Hidden decision making
- Missing documentation
- Ignoring risk information
- Slow governance processes
- Decisions based on hierarchy instead of evidence
- Failure to review outcomes
