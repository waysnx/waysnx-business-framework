# WBF-DOC-0140 – Enterprise Architecture Body of Knowledge

## Purpose

The Enterprise Architecture Body of Knowledge (EA-BoK) provides the comprehensive knowledge repository for the WaysNX Business Framework (WBF). It serves as the master reference that organizes, governs, and connects all architectural knowledge, principles, standards, reference models, implementation guidance, governance artifacts, reusable patterns, templates, and operational practices.

The EA-BoK enables architects, engineering teams, business stakeholders, governance bodies, and AI-assisted development tools to consistently discover, apply, and evolve enterprise architecture knowledge across all initiatives.

Rather than introducing new architectural concepts, this document consolidates and indexes the complete WBF knowledge ecosystem.

# 2. Definitions

## Enterprise Architecture Body of Knowledge (EA-BoK)

A structured and governed collection of enterprise architecture knowledge that defines how architecture is created, managed, governed, implemented, and continuously improved.

# 7. Knowledge Governance

Knowledge should be:

- Version controlled
- Reviewed periodically
- Traceable
- Reusable
- Technology neutral where possible
- Approved through governance
- Linked to related documents
- Archived rather than deleted
- Continuously improved

Governance responsibilities include:

- Architecture Review Board
- Enterprise Architects
- Domain Architects
- Engineering Leadership
- Product Owners

# 10. Best Practices

- Maintain a single enterprise knowledge repository.
- Reuse patterns before creating new solutions.
- Document major architectural decisions.
- Apply standard templates.
- Use architecture checklists consistently.
- Keep knowledge modular.
- Encourage continuous review and improvement.
- Enable AI-assisted knowledge discovery.
- Link related documents across domains.
- Measure framework adoption and effectiveness.

---

# 11. Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- Duplicate documentation
- Uncontrolled document versions
- Isolated architecture knowledge
- Missing governance
- Project-specific standards without approval
- Obsolete guidance remaining active
- Architecture decisions without documentation
- Ignoring reusable patterns
- Knowledge silos
- Unmaintained repositories
