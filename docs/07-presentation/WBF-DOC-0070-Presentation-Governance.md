# WBF-DOC-0070 – Presentation Governance

## Purpose

This specification defines the enterprise Presentation Governance model within the WaysNX Business Framework (WBF). It establishes the governance structure, standards, policies, review processes, ownership, compliance mechanisms, and continuous improvement practices required to ensure that all presentation-layer solutions remain consistent, maintainable, accessible, scalable, and aligned with enterprise architecture objectives.

Presentation Governance provides the organizational framework that ensures User Experience, User Interface, Design Systems, Accessibility, Responsive Design, Client Applications, Visualization, and Internationalization evolve in a coordinated and controlled manner across the enterprise.

This specification is technology independent and applies to all enterprise products, digital platforms, customer applications, internal systems, reusable libraries, and design assets.

- Dashboards

---

# 2. Definitions

### Presentation Governance

The policies, standards, decision-making processes, and organizational responsibilities that guide the evolution and maintenance of enterprise presentation architecture.

### Design Authority

The organizational body responsible for reviewing, approving, and governing presentation standards and design decisions.

### Architecture Review

A structured evaluation ensuring presentation solutions comply with enterprise architecture standards.

### Design System Governance

The management of reusable components, design tokens, themes, patterns, and visual standards.

### Compliance

The degree to which presentation implementations conform to established enterprise standards.

# 3. Objectives

Presentation Governance should:

- Ensure architectural consistency
- Promote component reuse
- Improve maintainability
- Standardize user experiences
- Enforce accessibility
- Support enterprise scalability
- Improve development efficiency
- Reduce duplication
- Enable continuous improvement
- Protect long-term architectural integrity

- Measurable
- Scalable
- Maintainable
- Continuously Improved

# 8. Governance Capabilities

Enterprise Presentation Governance should support:

## Standards Management

Maintain enterprise-wide presentation standards.

---

## Component Governance

Ensure reusable components meet architectural, usability, accessibility, and quality standards.

---

## Design System Evolution

Govern design token changes, component updates, theme management, and version evolution.

---

## Compliance Monitoring

Measure adherence to enterprise presentation standards.

---

## Architecture Decision Management

Document and manage significant presentation architecture decisions.

---

## Quality Assurance

Validate presentation quality through reviews, automated testing, accessibility validation, and usability assessments.

---

## Knowledge Management

Maintain architectural documentation, design guidelines, implementation standards, and reusable assets.

---

## Continuous Improvement

Continuously refine standards based on user feedback, technology evolution, and organizational learning.

# 9. Roles & Responsibilities

Presentation governance typically involves:

| Role | Responsibilities |
|------|------------------|
| Enterprise Architect | Presentation architecture direction |
| Solution Architect | Solution compliance |
| UX Architect | User experience standards |
| UI Architect | Component architecture |
| Design System Team | Component and token governance |
| Accessibility Lead | Accessibility compliance |

# 11. Best Practices

- Govern reusable assets centrally.
- Standardize design and development practices.
- Conduct regular architecture reviews.
- Automate compliance validation where possible.
- Maintain comprehensive documentation.
- Measure governance effectiveness using defined metrics.
- Encourage collaboration between design and engineering teams.
- Treat accessibility and usability as mandatory quality attributes.
- Continuously evolve standards based on business needs.

---

# 12. Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- Uncontrolled component creation
- Multiple competing design systems
- Inconsistent UI standards
- Governance only during project completion
- Missing architecture documentation
- Ignoring accessibility requirements
- Hardcoded presentation behaviors
- Duplicate visualization patterns
- Lack of ownership for presentation assets
