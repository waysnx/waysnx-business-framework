# WBF-DOC-0058 – Data Lifecycle Management

## Purpose

This specification defines the enterprise principles, governance, processes, and lifecycle for managing enterprise information throughout its existence within the WaysNX Business Framework (WBF).

Data Lifecycle Management (DLM) ensures that enterprise information is properly created, classified, stored, maintained, protected, shared, archived, retained, and securely disposed of according to business, operational, legal, and regulatory requirements. Effective lifecycle management maximizes business value while minimizing storage costs, operational risks, compliance issues, and security exposure.

This specification provides a technology-independent framework applicable across all enterprise information assets regardless of storage platform or deployment model.

- Reference Data
- Metadata

- Documents
- Reports
- Dashboards

- Backup Repositories

It governs enterprise information from creation through permanent disposal.

---

# 2. Definitions

### Data Lifecycle

The complete sequence of stages through which enterprise information passes from creation to secure disposal.

### Retention

The controlled preservation of information for a defined business, operational, legal, or regulatory period.

### Archival

The movement of inactive information to long-term storage while maintaining accessibility when required.

### Disposal

The secure destruction or permanent removal of information that has reached the end of its retention period.

### Information Value

The business, operational, legal, historical, or analytical importance of enterprise information throughout its lifecycle.

# 3. Objectives

Data Lifecycle Management should:

- Maximize business value
- Protect enterprise information
- Support compliance requirements
- Improve operational efficiency
- Reduce storage costs
- Minimize business risk
- Support business continuity
- Enable secure information disposal

# 4. Data Lifecycle Management Principles

Enterprise information should be:

- Business Driven
- Lifecycle Managed
- Governed
- Secure
- Traceable
- Retainable
- Recoverable
- Compliant
- Cost Effective
- Continuously Reviewed

Information should receive the level of protection and management appropriate to its business value throughout its lifecycle.

---

# 5. Data Lifecycle Stages

Enterprise information should progress through defined lifecycle stages.

### Planning

Business requirements define the purpose, ownership, classification, and expected lifecycle of information.

---

### Creation

Information is generated or acquired according to approved business processes.

---

### Classification

Information is categorized according to sensitivity, criticality, business value, and regulatory requirements.

---

### Active Usage

Information supports operational business processes and decision-making.

---

### Maintenance

Information is updated, validated, synchronized, and monitored throughout its operational life.

---

### Sharing

Information is exchanged securely between authorized systems, users, and business partners.

---

### Archival

Inactive information is transferred to long-term storage while remaining accessible when required.

---

### Retention

Archived information is preserved according to defined business and regulatory retention schedules.

---

### Disposal

Information reaching the end of its approved lifecycle is securely and permanently destroyed according to governance policies.

# 6. Lifecycle Policies

Enterprise lifecycle policies should define:

- Data Classification
- Ownership
- Creation Standards
- Retention Periods
- Archival Rules
- Backup Requirements
- Recovery Procedures
- Access Restrictions
- Disposal Procedures
- Periodic Reviews

Policies should be consistently applied across all enterprise information assets.

---

# 7. Data Retention & Disposal

Retention policies should consider:

- Business Requirements
- Legal Obligations
- Regulatory Requirements
- Contractual Obligations
- Audit Requirements
- Historical Value
- Analytical Value
- Operational Needs

Secure disposal should ensure that information cannot be reconstructed, recovered, or misused after its approved retention period has expired.

# 8. Lifecycle Governance

Lifecycle governance should define:

- Business Ownership
- Data Stewardship
- Lifecycle Policies
- Classification Standards
- Retention Governance
- Archival Governance
- Disposal Approval
- Audit Requirements
- Compliance Reviews
- Continuous Improvement

Lifecycle governance should ensure enterprise information remains protected and compliant throughout its existence.

---

# 9. Cross-Cutting Concerns

Data Lifecycle Management should consistently address:

- Business Architecture
- Data Architecture
- Data Storage Architecture
- Data Integration
- Metadata Management
- Master Data Management
- Data Quality
- Security
- Privacy
- Business Continuity
- Compliance
- Artificial Intelligence

Lifecycle management should align with enterprise governance across all architecture domains.

---

# 10. Best Practices

- Define lifecycle requirements before information is created.
- Classify enterprise information consistently.
- Assign ownership throughout the lifecycle.
- Archive inactive information appropriately.
- Apply standardized retention schedules.
- Periodically review retained information.
- Securely dispose of obsolete information.
- Continuously monitor lifecycle compliance.
- Automate lifecycle processes wherever practical.

---

# 11. Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- Retaining information indefinitely
- Missing retention policies
- Uncontrolled archival
- Disposing of information without approval
- Ignoring regulatory retention requirements
- Inconsistent lifecycle management
- Missing ownership during lifecycle transitions
- Poor disposal controls
- Treating all information equally regardless of business value
